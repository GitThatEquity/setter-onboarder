# The Role: Pre-Foreclosure Appointment Setter

## The job in one sentence

Call homeowners who have a foreclosure in progress, have a respectful conversation, and set an appointment so the homeowner can learn their options. The appointment is either an advisor call or an in-person sit-down with someone from the field team (door knockers normally do these), and the closer takes it from there.

## Why a setter exists

Homeowners in foreclosure are often scared, embarrassed, or avoiding the problem. The setter is the first friendly voice. The closer is the person who goes deep on the numbers and the options. Splitting the jobs lets the setter spend the day reaching people, and lets the closer spend their time on real conversations.

## What a setter does

- Calls homeowners from a prepared lead list.
- Opens the conversation, builds a little trust, and listens.
- Finds out the basics: is there a sale date, does the homeowner want to keep the home or sell, is anyone already helping them.
- Handles common objections using the 4-step pattern (`08_call_framework_and_objections.md`).
- Sets the appointment with a closer, or schedules a follow-up if they are not ready.
- Logs every contact attempt and every result, so nothing falls through the cracks.
- Stops contacting anyone who asks to stop.

## The levels (where this role can grow)

| Level | What they do | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Newbie Setter | Uses the Beginner Setter Foreclosure Script | Set Strategy Sessions |
| Closer-Level Setter | Uses the Closer-Level Setter Script | Set 5-options appointments |
| Full Closer | Closer-Level Setter script plus the Closing Appointment Script | Get contracts signed |

A new hire starts at Newbie Setter. The setter scripts are in `14_setter_scripts.md`. The Closer script is not in this folder because it is not a setter's job.

## How a setter's work is organized

**Starting out: the pool.** New setters begin in a pool, calling all the numbers. This is where you build experience and show you are doing the work.

**Moving up: your own group.** Once you show you're doing the work, being consistent, and putting in the time, you move up. You are placed in a specific group: a certain township in a certain county, working with a door knocker or a closer. Your calls focus there.

**The path.** Setter, then senior setter as your conversations get better, then closer. Pay rises with it: you start at 5%, move up to 10%, and closers are paid 20% of a deal. See `13_pay_and_hours.md`.

**There are no fixed thresholds for moving up.** It depends on how well you take on the conversations, the role, and setting appointments. The better you get at setting appointments, the faster you move up.

**Your leads.** Once you have built real rapport with a homeowner, that lead is **yours**. It is designated as yours, it goes into your follow-up, and nobody else should be calling it. This keeps the homeowner from getting calls from several people and keeps them talking to someone they already trust.

How long moving up usually takes, and who decides when you move from the pool to a group or from one level to the next, are not in this folder (see `12_not_covered.md`).

## What good looks like

- Real conversations, not just dials.
- Accurate notes: sale date, what the homeowner wants, who else is involved, the next step.
- Appointments that actually happen, with a homeowner who understood what they agreed to.
- Homeowners who felt respected, even when they said no.

The one requirement is a minimum of 2 hours of calling a day, 5 days a week (10 hours), done consistently. Setters choose when. Setters are independent contractors. See `13_pay_and_hours.md` for pay and hours. Specific call or appointment targets are not set in this folder (see `12_not_covered.md`).

## What is NOT a setter's job

- Making an offer or quoting a price.
- Explaining deal structures in detail, or promising any outcome.
- Giving legal advice.
- Promising the home can be saved.
- Pretending to be from the county, the lender, or the court.
- Deciding to remove a lead permanently on their own. That needs review.
