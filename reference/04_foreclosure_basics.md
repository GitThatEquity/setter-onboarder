# Foreclosure Basics (plain English)

This is education, not legal advice. When a question needs legal advice, the answer is: "I'm not an attorney, but I can help you understand the general process and point you toward the right resources."

Exact steps and timelines vary by county and by situation. If a new hire asks for a specific legal deadline, do not state one. Say it varies and that a licensed attorney or housing counselor can confirm.

## The funnel

```
Late on payments -> NOD stage (no sale date yet) -> NOTS stage (sheriff sale date set) -> Sale Date -> [Expired / Sold / Reinstated]
```

NOD and NOTS are our internal lead tags. Pennsylvania has sheriff sales, not trustee sales, so use "foreclosure" and "sheriff sale" with homeowners.

## The general mortgage foreclosure path in Pennsylvania

1. Missed payments.
2. Default notices.
3. An Act 91 notice, if it applies.
4. A legal complaint is filed.
5. Judgment.
6. A sheriff sale is scheduled.
7. The sheriff sale is held, unless it is stayed or postponed.
8. If the property sells for more than the debt, there may be surplus money to recover.

## Key ideas

**Sheriff sale.** The public sale of a property after a foreclosure judgment. It may or may not actually happen on the scheduled date.

**Stayed / postponed / continued.** The sale did not happen on that date. A stayed or continued sale can come back later. These show up on county lists.

**Reinstatement.** The homeowner catches up what they owe to bring the loan current. Their lender can tell them the reinstatement amount.

**Payoff.** The full amount needed to pay the whole loan off.

**Act 91.** A Pennsylvania homeowner protection process tied to mortgage foreclosure. Setters do not explain it legally. Good questions to ask: did they receive an Act 91 notice, did they respond, have they talked to a housing counselor? Encourage them to contact an approved counselor or an attorney.

**Loan modification.** A change to the loan terms, requested from the lender. It is not a guarantee, and "under review" is not the same as approved.

**Bankruptcy.** A legal process. A setter never says what it will or won't do. See the objection section in `08_call_framework_and_objections.md`.

**Owner-occupied.** The owner lives there. These are our main focus.

**Tax sale.** Property sold for unpaid taxes. This is different from a mortgage foreclosure. Always verify with the county office or legal counsel.

## What homeowners are usually feeling

Fear, embarrassment, confusion, overwhelm, distrust. The closer the sale date gets, the fewer options may remain, so a gentle, timely conversation matters.

## Where options come from (so you understand what the closer will discuss)

Keeping the home: calling the lender for a reinstatement quote, a repayment plan, a loan modification, a HUD-approved housing counselor, Act 91 options, legal aid or an attorney.

Possibly needing to sell: a traditional listing, a cash sale, taking over payments (Subject-To), seller financing, a hybrid offer, and other options the closer reviews. Setters do not present or recommend these. They set the appointment where the closer does.
