# Setter Scripts

These are the company's call scripts, written the way we actually talk to homeowners. Brackets like [first name] and [address] get filled in per call. Start with **Beginner**. Hold the other two for later in the week, once the new hire is comfortable.

**The rule behind every script:** your job is to have a kind, honest conversation and book an appointment. You do **not** make offers, quote prices, give advice, or try to close on the phone. The goal is simple: help the homeowner see their options before time runs out.

Use the scripts as a guide, not a speech. Say it in your own words. Slow down. Listen more than you talk.

Pace note: on day one, show only the Beginner script. Do not paste all three.

---

## How we describe what we do

We help homeowners save their homes and, if it comes down to it, help save their equity. We would much rather talk about how we can help them keep their home.

Do not introduce yourself as a real estate investor. Say "we help." Never say "I can save your home," and never promise the sale will stop. Do not invent other ways to describe the company.

## 1. Beginner Setter: Foreclosure (start here)

**Goal:** get a feel for where they are with three simple questions, then book the next step.

**Opener**
"Hi, is this [first name]? Hey [first name], my name is [your name]. I'm calling about the home on [street]. I know this might be a stressful time, so I'll be quick. We help homeowners who are facing foreclosure understand their options before the sale date. Is that something you're dealing with right now?"

If it's a bad time: "No problem. Would later today or tomorrow be better? Morning or afternoon?"

**Three questions** (ask them like a conversation, not a checklist)
1. "About how far behind are you on the mortgage, roughly?"
2. "Do you know if there's a sale date yet?"
3. "Have you looked into any options so far, like talking to the bank, or maybe selling?"

If they answer two of the three, you have enough. Move to the appointment.

**Book the appointment**
"Thank you for sharing that. I know that's not easy to talk about. Here's what I'd suggest. Let's get you in front of someone who can look at your whole situation and walk you through what's actually possible. It's free, there's no pressure, and it takes about 30 minutes. We can do it as a call with one of our advisors, or someone can come out and sit down with you in person. Which works better for you?"

Then: "Are mornings or afternoons easier?" Get a day and time. Confirm the best phone number for a call, or the address for an in-person visit.

**Close the call**
"Perfect. So we've got you down for [day] at [time]. Someone will check in with you the day before to confirm. Is there anyone else who should be there, like a spouse or a family member, so everyone's on the same page?"

"Thank you, [first name]. There's still time, and you have more options than you think."

**Simple objections**
- "Not interested." -> "I understand. Can I ask, do you already have a plan in place, or are you just not sure if anything can help?"
- "I'm not selling." -> "That's okay. Selling may not even be the answer. The whole point of the conversation is to look at all your options, including keeping the home. It's free and there's no pressure."
- "Call me back later." -> "Of course. Would morning or afternoon be better? Let me pencil in a time so I don't miss you."

## 2. Experienced Setter: Foreclosure (later in the week)

**Goal:** understand their situation a little deeper and book the appointment. Do not close on the phone.

**Opener**
"Hi, is this [first name]? Hey [first name], this is [your name]. I'm calling about the home on [street]. Did I catch you at a bad time?"

If bad time: "No problem. Is later today or tomorrow better, morning or afternoon?"

**Bridge**
"I'll be straight with you. I know you've probably gotten a lot of mail and calls lately, and I'm not calling to add to the pile. We help homeowners in foreclosure get clear on their options so they don't get blindsided by the sale date. And whenever it makes sense, we help people save their home. Would it be okay if I asked a few quick questions?"

**Understand where they are**
- "About how far behind are you on the mortgage right now?"
- "Do you know when the sale date is, or has one not been set yet?"
- "Have you talked to the bank or an attorney about anything yet, like a loan mod or getting caught up?"
- "Is it just you on the title, or is anyone else part of the decision?"

Listen for what they're feeling, not just the numbers. A short "That sounds really hard" goes a long way.

**Connect it to a next step**
"Thank you for being open with me. Depending on the numbers, there may be a few paths that could work. My job isn't to tell you what to do. It's to get you in front of someone who can look at the whole picture and tell you honestly what makes sense and what doesn't. No pressure, no obligation."

**Book the appointment**
"Would it help to sit down for about 30 to 45 minutes? We can do it by phone with one of our advisors, or someone can come out and meet you in person. By the end, you'll know exactly where you stand. Do mornings or afternoons usually work better for you?"

Pin down the day and time. Confirm the phone number or address. Repeat it back.

**Confirm**
"Great, so that's [day] at [time]. Someone will reach out the day before to confirm. Is there anyone else who should be part of that conversation, like a spouse or a family member?"

**Objections**
- "I'm not interested." -> "Totally fair. Can I just ask, is it that you've got a plan in place, or more that you're not sure if anyone can really help?"
- "I'm working with the bank." -> "That's good, and I hope it works out. This just gives you a backup in case the bank's timeline doesn't line up. One thing worth checking: has anyone told you in writing that the sale date is paused? Would [day/time] work for a quick conversation?"
- "I'm not selling." -> "I hear you, and you may not have to. A lot of the time the goal is helping you keep the home. That's part of what the conversation is for. It's free. Does [day] work?"

## 3. Cold Call (later in the week)

**Opener (first ten seconds)**
"Hi, is this [first name]? Hey [first name], my name is [your name]. I saw that the home on [street] came up in the foreclosure filings, and I wanted to reach out personally instead of sending another letter. Did I catch you at a bad time?"

If bad time: "No problem. When's better today, morning or afternoon?"

**Bridge**
"I'll keep it short. We help homeowners facing foreclosure save their homes, and if it comes down to it, help save their equity. Before the sale date, the first step is just getting clear on what your options are. I'm not here to pressure you. I just want to understand your situation and see if we can help. Is that okay?"

**Four questions**
1. "About how far behind are you right now?"
2. "Do you know the sale date, or has one been set?"
3. "Have you been working with the bank on anything, like a loan mod or getting caught up?"
4. "Is it just you making decisions on the home, or is someone else involved?"

If they're behind, don't have a plan, or the sale date is close, that's a real need. Offer the appointment.

**Book it**
"Based on what you're telling me, I think there are options worth walking through. I'd like to set up a quick 30-minute conversation. It can be a call with one of our advisors, or someone can come out and meet you in person, whatever feels easiest. Which would you prefer, and does [day] or [day] work better?"

Get the day and time. Confirm the number or address. Repeat it back. Then: "Is there anyone else who should be there, like a spouse or family member?"

**Objections**
- "I'm not interested / I'm not selling." -> "I understand. And selling may not even be the right answer. Part of the conversation is figuring out what is, including how to keep your home. Would [day] or [day] work for a quick, no-pressure talk?"
- "I'm working with the bank." -> "That's good, and I don't want to get in the way. I just want you to have a backup if their timeline doesn't work out. Would [day] work?"
- "Just send me something in the mail." -> "I can. But honestly, a conversation helps more, because I can answer your questions right away. Would [day] or [day] work for a quick call?"
- "How did you get my number?" -> "The foreclosure filing is public record. I'd rather call you directly than send another letter. I'm not here to embarrass you. I just don't want you to get to the sale date without knowing your options."

**If they say no: book the next check-in**
"I understand. Can I check back with you in a few days, closer to your sale date, just to see where things stand? Mornings or afternoons better?"

## The two kinds of appointment

A setter can offer the homeowner either one, whichever fits them:

1. **Advisor call.** One of our advisors reaches out and talks it through by phone.
2. **In-person sit-down.** Someone from our team goes out into the field and sits down with the homeowner. Our door knockers normally do these.

If the homeowner prefers one, book that one. Which specific person takes a given appointment is assigned by the owner. If a new hire asks who, say it is not in the folder and to ask the owner.

---

## Using the scripts well

- Say it in your own voice. Slow down.
- Ask the questions like a conversation, not a checklist. Listen to the answers.
- Do not argue with an objection. Use the 4-step pattern in `08_call_framework_and_objections.md`.
- Do not describe deal options or give any price. That happens at the appointment.
- If they say the sale is stopped, or mention bankruptcy or an attorney, follow `09_boundaries_and_escalation.md`.
- Log every call, including no answers and voicemails.
