# Pay and Hours

This is what the owner has confirmed. Anything not on this page is not known to the onboarder. Do not fill gaps.

## How the company makes money (so pay makes sense)

32 East puts a distressed house under contract, then finds a buyer for it. The difference between the price 32 East agreed to pay and the price the buyer pays is the **assignment fee**.

Owner's example: 32 East gets a house under contract at $300,000 and the buyer pays $330,000. The assignment fee is $30,000.

## How setter pay works

- Setters are paid a **percentage of the assignment fee**.
- **You start at 5%.** As you show you're doing the work, being consistent, and putting in the time, you **move up to 10%**.
- Getting better at the conversations moves you from setter to senior setter, and then to **closer, who is paid 20% of a deal**.
- **There are no fixed thresholds for moving up.** It depends on how well you take on the conversations, the role, and setting appointments. The better you get at setting appointments, the faster you move up.
- Everyone is paid from **the same assignment fee**. Owner's example: 32 East buys a house for $300,000 and sells it for $330,000, so the fee is $30,000. If the setter earns 10% ($3,000) and the closer earns 20% ($6,000), the rest ($21,000) goes to the company. A setter at 5% would earn $1,500.
- Closing costs are paid by the buyer. Any other expenses on a deal come out of the company's share, which is why the company's share is the largest. A setter does not need to track expenses.
- That example is math, not a promise or a typical result. Many leads never become a deal, and every deal is different.
- **Pay happens when the deal closes through title.** Everyone is paid out through the title company at closing. The owner does not pay people directly or disburse funds himself.
- No deal closing means no pay for that lead. There is no hourly wage described here.

**Which percentage applies to a specific person, and when they move up, is set by the owner.** Do not promise a new hire a raise or a date. Say how the path works and tell them to confirm their own rate with the owner.

## When a deal counts for a setter

A deal counts for a setter when **that setter talks to the homeowner and sets the appointment, a closer takes the appointment, and the closer closes the deal.** That locks the deal to that setter and that closer.

It is **first come, first served.** The setter who sets the appointment that gets closed is tied to the deal.

Owner's example: Jill (setter) calls a homeowner, has the conversation, and sets the appointment. Fred (closer) takes the appointment and closes the deal. That is Jill's deal. If Jessica (another setter) also calls the same homeowner and makes an appointment that same day, it is not Jessica's deal, because Jill and Fred had already locked it.

This fits with how leads are owned: once a setter has built rapport with a homeowner, the lead is designated as theirs and goes into their follow-up, and other setters should not call it. See `02_the_role.md`.

What this means for a new hire: log every call and appointment clearly and promptly. The setter who actually sets the appointment that closes is the one who is tied to the deal.

## Everyone signs a JV agreement

To be paid through title, **everybody signs a JV (joint venture) agreement.** The terms of that agreement are not in this folder. Do not describe or interpret them. Tell the new hire the owner will provide it, that they should read it carefully and ask the owner about anything unclear, and that they can have their own attorney review it if they want. Do not give legal advice about it.

## Setters are independent contractors

Setters are **independent contractors, not employees**. Questions about taxes, how to report income, or what that means legally are not for the onboarder to answer. Say it is not in the folder and suggest they ask the owner and a tax professional of their own. Never give tax or legal advice.

## Hours

- **Minimum: 2 hours a day of phone calls, 5 days a week (10 hours a week).** That is the bare minimum and it is required.
- The 10 hours means **calling time**, not just time logged in or time "available."
- Calling time is tracked in **Go High Level or Salesforce**. Which one a given setter uses is set by the owner.
- Setters choose when to call. The one expectation is **consistency**. The schedule is intentionally loose, with no set shifts.

## Not confirmed in this folder (ask the owner)

- How long a typical move up takes, and who decides. There are no fixed thresholds, so do not invent any.
- What happens if the first appointment falls through and a different setter later books a new one for the same homeowner. The owner has only described the case where the first setter's appointment is the one that closes.
- The terms of the JV agreement.
- Which of Go High Level or Salesforce a given setter uses, and how to get access.
- Time off.

Suggested way for a new hire to ask: "Can you confirm my percentage and send me the JV agreement? I'd like both in writing."

## What this means for a new hire (how to say it)

- Be honest that early weeks can pay little or nothing, because pay comes at closing and deals take time. Do not promise income. The owner can speak to what to expect.
- Two steady hours a day matters more than one big burst.
- Good notes and honest conversations lead to appointments, and appointments are what can turn into deals.

## Never

- Never promise earnings or say a specific amount a setter will make.
- Never state the percentage as a guarantee.
- Never give tax or legal advice.
