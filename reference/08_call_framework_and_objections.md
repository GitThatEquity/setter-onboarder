# Call Framework and Objections

The actual scripts are in `14_setter_scripts.md`. This file covers the thinking behind a call and how to handle what homeowners say.

## What a good call is trying to do

1. Be a calm, kind voice.
2. Find out where they are: is there a sale date, what do they want, is anyone helping.
3. Give them a little clarity.
4. Offer a simple next step: an appointment with a closer, or a follow-up.

A "no" is okay. Leave them better informed and respected.

## The 4-step pattern for any objection

1. **Acknowledge.** Validate what they said.
2. **Clarify.** Ask questions to understand their real situation.
3. **Reality check.** Timeline, math, and daily-life implications, said gently.
4. **Next step.** For a setter: a strategy session or appointment. (A closer's next step is an appointment or contract.)

Quick version used in training: Acknowledge, Normalize, Clarify, Offer a next step.

Example: "That makes sense. Most people would feel the same way. Can I ask what part you're unsure about, the timeline, the numbers, or whether this is even the right option?"

## The 3 core objections to master

**1. "I've got it taken care of."**
Ask: "Has anyone given you anything in writing that actually pauses the sale date?"

**2. "I'm filing bankruptcy."**
Ask: "Did they tell you in writing the sale is paused and it's approved? Do you actually have the money they need up front?"
Never say what bankruptcy will or won't do. If they want legal guidance, they should talk to a licensed attorney.

**3. "I'm doing a loan modification."**
Ask: "Have you finished the packet or are they still 'reviewing your file'? Did the payment go up?"

These work because they are respectful, they are questions and not arguments, and they check whether the problem is actually solved.

## Other common objections (company responses)

- **"I'm not interested."** "I understand. A lot of people feel that way at first, especially when a stranger contacts them. I'm not here to pressure you. I just wanted you to know there may still be options depending on where things stand." Optional: "Would it be okay if I left my information in case you need it later?"
- **"I need to think about it."** "Totally fair. The only thing I'd say is the timeline keeps moving while you think. Would it help to get clear on the options now so you're not forced into a decision later?"
- **"I need to talk to my spouse or family."** "Absolutely, they should be part of it. What if we set up a quick call with everyone so nobody feels left out?"
- **"You're just trying to buy my house."** "I understand why it could feel that way. Buying may or may not be the right answer. Some people want to keep the home, some need time, some need resources. I'd rather understand what you want first."
- **"How did you find me?"** "Foreclosure filings are public record. I know that can feel uncomfortable, and I'm not here to embarrass you. Once the timeline gets close, options can disappear quickly."
- **"I don't want to sell."** "You may not have to. If your goal is to keep the home, the first step is figuring out what it would take to reinstate, modify, or work something out with the lender." Then: "Do you know the reinstatement amount yet?"
- **"I'm embarrassed."** "I get it. You're not the only person dealing with this. Life happens: job loss, medical issues, taxes, family situations. I'm not here to judge you."
- **"I don't know what I owe."** "That's okay. The next step is requesting a reinstatement amount and payoff from the lender."
- **"The bank said they're working with me."** "That may be true, and I hope so. What I'd want to verify is whether the sale date is officially postponed or stopped. Sometimes a file is in review but the sale date is still active."
- **"My attorney is handling it."** "That's good. I don't want to interfere. Just make sure you know the current sale date and what the attorney needs from you."
- **"Can you stop the sale?"** "I can't promise that. What I can do is help you look at where things stand and what options may still be available. Sometimes a sale can be avoided, but it depends on the facts."
- **"Is this a scam?"** "That's a fair question. You can look up my business, ask questions, and involve an attorney or trusted advisor before signing anything."

## Questions to use when someone stalls

- "What part feels unclear?"
- "What are you most worried about?"
- "Is your goal still to avoid the sale?"
- "Do you want to keep the home or are you leaning toward selling?"
- "Who else needs to be involved in this decision?"
- "What happens if nothing changes before the sale date?"
- "Would clarity help, even if you decide not to work with us?"

## Objection tracks (what the company runs after)

- "BK will save me": BK objection track (10 to 14 days).
- "The mod will fix it": loan mod objection track.
- "I'll reinstate": post-reinstatement follow-up track.
- "I'll list and get more": market reality track.

New hires do not run these on day one. Know that they exist.

## What not to do

Do not argue, debate, shame, pressure, over-talk, use scare tactics, make guarantees, or tell them what they "have to" do.

Do slow down, listen, ask better questions, clarify the timeline, offer resources, and respect their decision.

## Follow-up rules in short

Keep messages short. Lead with care. Remind them of the timeline. Offer one simple next step. Track every attempt. Stop if they clearly ask you to stop. Never pretend to be from the county, lender, or court, and never say you can save the home.
